Buenos Aires was built to be magnificent. The broad boulevards, the extravagant French and Italian architecture, the parks and plazas — this is a city that in its early twentieth-century heyday was genuinely one of the wealthiest in the world. That prosperity faded, but the beauty and the appetite for life did not. What remains is one of South America's most seductive cities: cosmopolitan, cultured, passionate, and permanently in the grip of its own tragic music.

### San Telmo and the Tango Roots

**San Telmo** is Buenos Aires's oldest neighborhood — cobblestone streets, nineteenth-century townhouses, antique markets, and the original home of tango. The music emerged here among the immigrant working classes in the late 1800s, a fusion of African rhythms, European polka, and Cuban habanera that somehow produced something entirely new. The **San Telmo Market** on Sundays draws street tango performers to its outdoor plaza — watching a couple dance on the pavement, with no stage or safety net, to a bandoneón and violin, is an intimate and genuinely moving experience. For a seated milonga, the **El Viejo Almacén** and **La Catedral** offer very different but equally authentic experiences.

### La Boca and Puerto Madero

The neighbourhood of **La Boca** is famous for its brightly painted corrugated-iron houses along **Caminito** — a pedestrianised street that is more open-air museum than living neighbourhood these days, but still visually exuberant. Just beyond it, the **Boca Juniors** football stadium (La Bombonera) is a pilgrimage site for football fans worldwide. By contrast, the renovated waterfront of **Puerto Madero** — former industrial docks turned into a gleaming neighbourhood of restaurants, hotels, and a pedestrian bridge designed by Santiago Calatrava — shows the modernising face of Buenos Aires with considerable style.

### Gotchas & Tips

- **Eating Hours**: Buenos Aires operates on a very late schedule. Restaurants do not fill up until 9 PM at the earliest; 11 PM is normal. A steak dinner at 10 PM at a traditional **parrilla** (grill restaurant) is the correct Buenos Aires experience. Bring patience and a good appetite.
- **The Currency Complexity**: Argentina has historically had a parallel exchange rate. Research the current situation carefully before arriving — the official and unofficial rates can differ substantially, affecting your purchasing power significantly.
- **Neighbourhood Logic**: Buenos Aires rewards neighbourhood exploration. **Palermo**, with its designer boutiques and restaurants; **Recoleta**, with its grand cemetery and café culture; and **Villa Crespo**, the local alternative to Palermo — each has its own distinct character.

### Highlights

- **Recoleta Cemetery**: One of the world's great cemeteries — an elaborate city of marble mausoleums where Argentina's elite, including Evita Perón, are interred. Wander it for hours.
- **Teatro Colón**: One of the finest opera houses in the world, with acoustics that rival Vienna and La Scala. The guided tour alone is worth the visit.
- **Café Tortoni**: The city's oldest café, open since 1858, where tango concerts and chess games coexist with legendary coffee and medialunas.
- **Tigre Delta**: An hour from downtown Buenos Aires, the Paraná Delta is a network of islands, waterways, and weekend houses reached by launch — a completely different Argentina.

Buenos Aires is a city that insists on beauty even in difficulty. That insistence — stubborn, passionate, and deeply human — is what makes it unforgettable.
