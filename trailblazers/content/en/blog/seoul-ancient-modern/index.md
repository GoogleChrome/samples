![Seoul, South Korea](cover.jpg)

Seoul moves at a speed that other cities aspire to. The transit system is immaculate and frequent, the convenience stores are open all night and genuinely convenient, the food culture is innovative and deeply traditional simultaneously, and the city's relationship with its own past — five royal palaces from the Joseon dynasty intact within the metropolitan area — is one of the most graceful urban negotiations between old and new anywhere in Asia. Seoul is the city that proves you do not have to choose between heritage and ambition.

### Gyeongbokgung Palace and Bukchon

**Gyeongbokgung Palace** — the main royal palace of the Joseon dynasty — was first built in 1395 and is surrounded by over three hundred reconstructed traditional buildings on a site that originally held five hundred. The changing of the guard ceremony at the main gate (10 AM and 2 PM daily) is performed with a precision and colour that rewards attention. Immediately north, the **Bukchon Hanok Village** is a residential neighbourhood of preserved traditional Korean houses (hanok) on a hillside between the two great palaces — narrow lanes, wooden eaves, stone walls, and the best coffee shops in the city occupying converted hanok interiors.

### Hongdae and the Han River

**Hongdae** — the neighbourhood around Hongik University — is where Seoul's youth culture runs at its most concentrated: indie music venues, street dancers, independent fashion designers, and street food stalls open until 4 AM. The contrast with the palace district thirty minutes away by metro is absolute and is precisely the point about Seoul. The **Han River** that bisects the city is something that most visitors underuse — the river parks (Yeouido, Banpo) fill with Seoulites cycling, having picnics, and watching the **Banpo Bridge Rainbow Fountain** (the world's longest bridge fountain) every evening. Joining them is one of the best ways to understand the city.

### Gotchas & Tips

- **T-money Card**: The transit card that works on metro, bus, and taxis is a non-negotiable Seoul tool — available at any convenience store (GS25, CU, 7-Eleven) for a small deposit, top-up-able anywhere, and giving slightly cheaper fares than paying cash.
- **The Jjimjilbang Experience**: A **jjimjilbang** (Korean public bathhouse) is the essential Seoul experience that most tourists miss. You pay entry (around 10,000–15,000 won), get a uniform, and access saunas at different temperatures, common rooms, and sleeping areas. Many are open 24 hours. It is extraordinary and entirely accessible to non-Korean speakers.
- **Korean BBQ Protocol**: At a **samgyeopsal** (pork belly BBQ) restaurant, the grill is at your table, the side dishes (banchan) are communal and refillable, and the soju or makgeolli is essential. Order the perilla leaf wraps and the doenjang jjigae alongside.

### Highlights

- **Namsan Tower and Seoul City Wall**: The hike up to N Seoul Tower through the remaining section of the 600-year-old city wall is a meditative experience in the middle of a megacity.
- **Gwangjang Market**: Seoul's oldest market, with raw silk, vintage fabric, and a legendary food court where the bindaetteok (mung bean pancakes) and mayak kimbap (sesame rice rolls) are the defining snacks.
- **DMZ Day Trip**: The Demilitarised Zone on the North Korean border, reachable on a guided tour from Seoul in two hours, is one of the most historically charged landscapes on earth.
- **Insadong**: The traditional arts district, with galleries, tea houses, and the best traditional craft shops in the city — buy a hanji paper product, a celadon teacup, or a handmade ink stick.

Seoul is a city that surprises on every visit. The layers keep revealing themselves, and the depth keeps increasing the more time you give it.
