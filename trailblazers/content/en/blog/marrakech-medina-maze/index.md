The moment you pass through one of the ancient gates in the rose-red ramparts of Marrakech's medina, the modern world simply evaporates. Narrow alleyways twist in every direction — one moment you are surrounded by pyramids of saffron and dried roses, the next you stumble into a hidden courtyard where a fountain murmurs beneath a canopy of carved cedar. The medina was built to disorient outsiders, and it succeeds spectacularly.

### The Souks and Djemaa el-Fna

The souks are organized roughly by trade — leatherworkers clustered around the tanneries near Bab Debbagh, brass lanterns hanging in the metalworkers' quarter, mountains of woven carpets in the textile district. But the logic dissolves quickly as you go deeper. The gravitational center of everything is **Djemaa el-Fna**, a UNESCO-recognized square that transforms through the day: juice sellers and snake charmers in the afternoon, then a vast outdoor feast of grilled meats and storytellers by night. The square is best viewed from the rooftop terrace of a café on its edge, mint tea in hand.

### Riads and the Art of Slowing Down

Hidden behind plain street-facing walls, **riads** — traditional courtyard houses — are Marrakech's greatest secret. Staying in one changes the entire rhythm of your visit. You step off a chaotic lane straight into a world of orange trees, mosaic tiles, and absolute quiet. The **Bahia Palace** offers a glimpse into the grandeur of the riad tradition on a palatial scale: room after room of zellij tilework and painted ceilings that took Moroccan craftsmen years to complete.

### Gotchas & Tips

- **Guides and Navigation**: The medina genuinely requires either a local guide for your first day or at least offline maps downloaded in advance. Well-meaning "guides" who approach you on the street are usually commission-driven — a firm smile and a confident stride go a long way.
- **Bargaining Etiquette**: Haggling is expected in the souks but has its own unspoken rules. Starting at a third of the asking price and being willing to walk away is usually effective. Never make an offer you are not prepared to accept.
- **The Heat**: Between May and September, Marrakech's heat is serious. Carry water, wear loose linen, and plan active sightseeing for early morning or late afternoon.

### Highlights

- **Djemaa el-Fna at Night**: One of the world's great public spectacles — chaotic, joyful, and completely unforgettable.
- **Saadian Tombs**: A sixteenth-century royal burial site discovered only in 1917 — the carved plasterwork is extraordinary.
- **Maison de la Photographie**: Remarkable archive of historical photos of Morocco that reframes everything you see outside.
- **Café des Épices**: The best rooftop view over the spice market, with an honest kitchen to match.

Marrakech rewards patience and punishes haste. Surrender your map, follow the smell of bread from a wood-fired oven, and let the city find you.
