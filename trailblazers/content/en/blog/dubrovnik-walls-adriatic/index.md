![Dubrovnik, Croatia](cover.jpg)

From the sea, Dubrovnik looks like a city that was placed there by someone who wanted to create the perfect Mediterranean composition. The limestone walls glow amber in afternoon light, terracotta rooftops cascade toward the water, and the Adriatic is a shade of blue that resists being named. Within those walls, the old town has been immaculately restored after the bombardment of the 1990s — a resilience that makes walking its streets feel like more than tourism.

### The City Walls

The **City Walls** circuit is two kilometres of uninterrupted rampart walkway above the sea, the rooftops, and the outer harbour — one of the finest walks in the world. The walls average four metres thick and rise to twenty-five metres in places. On the seaward side, the Adriatic stretches to the horizon. On the landward side, the entire old town spreads out below you like a map. Buy your tickets early — the walls get crowded by mid-morning, and doing the circuit in the wrong direction (clockwise is better) means fighting the flow. The views from **Fortress Minčeta** at the highest point are extraordinary.

### Stradun and the Hidden Lanes

The broad limestone promenade of **Stradun** is Dubrovnik's spine — polished smooth by centuries of feet and almost uncomfortably photogenic. But the real character of the old town lives in the steeply climbing lanes on either side, where local families still live above their washing lines. The **Buza Bar** — a bar cut directly into the cliff face, accessible only through a hole in the city wall — may be the most dramatically situated drinking spot in Europe. Swim from the rocks below, climb back up, and order a cold Croatian beer.

### Gotchas & Tips

- **Overtourism is Real**: July and August turn Dubrovnik's old town into a slow-moving mass of bodies. Visit in May, early June, or October for something approaching a normal experience. Even then, arrive at the walls at 8 AM.
- **The Cable Car Alternative**: The cable car up to **Mount Srđ** above the city takes less than four minutes and offers a view that contextualises everything — the old town, the islands, the mainland mountains. The old Napoleonic fort at the summit has an excellent museum on the 1991–92 siege.
- **Island Escape**: The ferry to **Lokrum Island** takes twelve minutes. The island is forested, peacock-populated, and has a saltwater lake for swimming — a complete antidote to Stradun on a busy afternoon.

### Highlights

- **City Walls at Sunrise**: The only time they belong entirely to you.
- **Fort Lovrijenac**: The free-standing fortress outside the walls with harbour views that appear in Game of Thrones — visit for the history, stay for the Shakespeare performances in summer.
- **Oysters at the Pelješac Peninsula**: The road north passes through Croatia's finest wine country and oyster-farming villages — worth a full day trip.
- **Stari Grad (Hvar)**: The nearby island of Hvar is a ninety-minute ferry ride away and offers a very different, more cosmopolitan Adriatic experience.

Dubrovnik earns its reputation ten times over, even through the crowds. It is one of the places that genuinely lives up to every photograph.
