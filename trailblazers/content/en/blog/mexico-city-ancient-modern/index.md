Mexico City was built on a lake. The Aztec capital of Tenochtitlán rose from the waters of Lake Texcoco on artificial islands connected by causeways, and when the Spanish conquered it in 1521 they built their colonial capital directly on top. The city has been subsiding into its lacustrine foundations ever since — colonial buildings list at angles that would alarm a builder elsewhere — but the layers of civilisation compressed beneath its streets are what make Mexico City one of the most historically dense and culturally extraordinary cities in the Americas.

### The Zócalo and Ancient Mexico

The **Zócalo** — formally the Plaza de la Constitución — is one of the world's great public squares: a vast flat space flanked by the Metropolitan Cathedral (built partly from Aztec temple stones), the National Palace (covered in Diego Rivera murals), and the remains of the **Templo Mayor**. The Templo Mayor was the ceremonial heart of Tenochtitlán, excavated from beneath modern streets only in 1978 after a worker accidentally struck it with a pickaxe. The adjacent **Museo del Templo Mayor** displays the extraordinary finds and explains the Aztec cosmology with clarity and depth that few museums achieve.

### Roma and Condesa — The Modern City

The neighbourhoods of **Roma** and **Condesa** represent Mexico City's contemporary creative life: tree-lined avenues of Art Nouveau and Art Deco buildings converted into excellent restaurants, cafés, bookshops, and galleries. The morning market in the **Mercado Medellín** is where the neighbourhood cooks shop — the fresh produce, cheese, and flower stalls are extraordinary. The **Museo Nacional de Antropología** in Chapultepec Park is arguably the finest anthropological museum in the world — the Aztec Sun Stone alone justifies the visit, but the collections spanning all of Mexico's pre-Columbian cultures are overwhelming in their scope and quality.

### Gotchas & Tips

- **Altitude Reality**: Mexico City sits at 2,240 metres — high enough to cause altitude sickness in some visitors. Drink plenty of water, avoid alcohol the first day, and take the first 24 hours gently regardless of how you feel.
- **The Taco Geography**: Mexico City's taco culture is both overwhelming and magnificent. **Al pastor** from a trompo spit (best in the Condesa area) and **consomé de barbacoa** on Sunday morning in a traditional market are the two essential experiences. Avoid tourist-oriented taco chains.
- **Traffic and Transport**: Mexico City's metro is the second-largest in Latin America, remarkably cheap, and usually the fastest way across the city. The traffic above ground is genuinely challenging. Download the Metro app for navigation.

### Highlights

- **Frida Kahlo Museum (La Casa Azul)**: Kahlo's home in Coyoacán is one of the world's great artistic pilgrimages — vivid, intimate, and genuinely moving. Book tickets weeks in advance.
- **Xochimilco Trajineras**: The ancient canal network south of the city, where colourful flat-bottomed boats carry families and musicians through the chinampas (floating gardens) every weekend.
- **Lucha Libre Wrestling**: A Friday night match at the **Arena México** — masks, acrobatics, theatrical villains — is one of the great Mexican cultural spectacles.
- **Palacio de Bellas Artes**: The Art Nouveau/Art Deco opera house and cultural centre contains Rivera, Siqueiros, and Orozco murals of staggering quality.

Mexico City does not condescend. It offers everything — history, food, art, chaos, beauty — and trusts you to find your own way through it.
