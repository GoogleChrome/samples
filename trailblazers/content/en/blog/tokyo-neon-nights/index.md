![Tokyo, Japan](cover.jpg)

Tokyo is a city of layers, a place where a 400-year-old shrine can sit comfortably in the shadow of a skyscraper that looks like it was built in 2050. As a photographer, I love the way the city switches from the neon chaos of Shinjuku to the deep, mossy quiet of a temple garden in the blink of an eye.

### Chaos and Calm

We started our day at the **Shibuya Crossing**. There is a strange, meditative quality to being in the middle of a thousand people all moving in different directions and not bumping into anyone. From there, we went on a "tech hunt" in **Akihabara**, where the sheer scale of the gadgetry and anime culture is overwhelming in the best way possible.

In the evening, we explored the **Tsukiji Outer Market**. While the inner wholesale market has moved, the outer market is still a maze of tiny stalls selling the freshest seafood I've ever seen. We finished our night in the **Golden Gai**—a tiny area of Shinjuku with over 200 miniature bars, some only seating four or five people. It’s the best place to share a drink and a story with a local.

### The Art of the Bowl

Tokyo is arguably the food capital of the world. We stood in line for 45 minutes for a bowl of **Ramen at Ichiran**, and it was worth every second—rich tonkotsu broth, perfectly chewy noodles, and a spicy red sauce that ties it all together. For a faster experience, we went to a **conveyor belt sushi** place in Ueno. The speed and precision with which the chefs work is a performance in itself.

### Gotchas & Tips

- **Suica is Life**: Get a Suica or Pasmo card (or add it to your Apple/Google Wallet). You can use it for the subway, buses, and even vending machines and convenience stores.
- **Cash is Still King**: While many places take cards, many small bars and traditional ramen shops only take cash. Always have some Yen on you.
- **The Garbage Mystery**: There are almost no public trash cans in Tokyo. Carry a small bag and be prepared to take your trash back to your hotel.

### Highlights

- **Shibuya Crossing**: The world's most famous intersection.
- **Golden Gai Bars**: A glimpse into the tiny, intimate soul of Shinjuku.
- **Ichiran Ramen**: The gold standard for a reason.
- **Meiji Jingu Shrine**: A peaceful forest in the middle of the city.

Tokyo is a city that surprises you at every turn. It’s a place that asks you to be curious, be polite, and be hungry.
