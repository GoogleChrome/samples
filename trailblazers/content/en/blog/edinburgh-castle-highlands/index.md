Edinburgh is one of those cities that announces itself. The volcanic crag of Castle Rock, rising ninety metres above the city with the castle on its crown, creates an urban drama that no amount of familiarity can diminish. Below it, the Old Town's medieval tenements — some of the tallest buildings in the pre-industrial world — press together along the Royal Mile, while the Georgian New Town spreads in rational grids to the north. It is a city of two personalities, and both of them are excellent.

### Edinburgh Castle and the Royal Mile

**Edinburgh Castle** commands the highest ground in the city and has done so continuously since at least the twelfth century. The Scottish Crown Jewels inside — older than those of England — are among the most moving royal artefacts in Britain, partly because of the extraordinary story of their concealment and rediscovery. The **Royal Mile** descends from the castle to the **Palace of Holyroodhouse**, the Scottish residence of the monarch. Along it, the narrow closes and wynds that branch off to either side are a medieval urban labyrinth where every dark stairwell seems to promise a ghost story.

### Arthur's Seat and the Pentland Hills

The **Arthur's Seat** volcanic hill rises from **Holyrood Park** in the middle of the city to 251 metres — a proper hill walk, not a gentle stroll, accessible via several routes in about an hour of moderate climbing. The view from the summit encompasses the entire city, the Firth of Forth, Fife beyond it, and on clear days the Highland peaks. For more serious walking, the **Pentland Hills** begin just twenty minutes south of the city centre and offer half-day to full-day ridge walks above the suburban sprawl.

### Gotchas & Tips

- **Fringe Festival Timing**: Edinburgh in August is extraordinary — the world's largest arts festival transforms the city into a permanent outdoor performance. It is also more expensive and harder to navigate. Book accommodation six months ahead, or visit in October for the jazz festival with a fraction of the crowds.
- **The Weather Reality**: Scottish weather is not predictable. Pack for all four seasons regardless of the forecast — a waterproof layer is never wrong.
- **Whisky Education**: The **Scotch Whisky Experience** on the Royal Mile is a good orientation, but for genuine distillery history, day trips to **Glenkinchie** (thirty minutes south) or the ferry to **Islay** for peaty single malts are incomparable.

### Highlights

- **National Museum of Scotland**: Free, extraordinary, and one of the finest museums in Britain — the collections range from a Pictish stone to Dolly the sheep.
- **Greyfriars Kirkyard**: The atmospheric graveyard that inspired the Harry Potter names and contains some of the most storied graves in Scotland.
- **Stockbridge Sunday Market**: Edinburgh's finest neighbourhood, with a Sunday market and the Water of Leith walkway behind it.
- **Dean Village**: A ten-minute walk from Princes Street, this restored medieval milling village beside a river feels genuinely like another century.

Edinburgh rewards the curious and the willing to walk uphill. The city gives back in proportion to the effort put in.
