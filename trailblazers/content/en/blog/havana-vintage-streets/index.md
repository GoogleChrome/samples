Havana is a city frozen mid-sentence. The 1950s American cars — Buicks and Chevrolets in candy colours, their engines replaced with Russian diesel — cruise the Malecón seawall as if nothing unusual has happened since Eisenhower was president. The colonial facades of Habana Vieja are simultaneously crumbling and magnificent. And through all of it, the people of Havana move with a warmth and music and resourcefulness that is simply not reproducible anywhere else.

### Habana Vieja — the Old City

**Habana Vieja**, Havana's colonial old town, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site undergoing slow but genuine restoration. The four main plazas — **Plaza de Armas**, Plaza Vieja, Plaza de San Francisco, and Plaza de la Catedral — each have their own character and history. The Catedral de San Cristóbal is a baroque masterwork that took nearly a century to complete. In the evenings, the streets fill with musicians playing son and bolero — not for tourists exactly, but not indifferent to them either. The rum is excellent and remarkably inexpensive.

### The Malecón and Vedado

The **Malecón** is Havana's great outdoor living room — a six-kilometre seawall boulevard where fishermen, lovers, chess players, and philosophers all coexist. Walk it in both directions at different times of day. The area of **Vedado** beyond the Malecón contains a different Havana: the tree-lined avenues, mid-century hotels, and the extraordinary **Colón Cemetery**, where the elaborate marble mausoleums of Havana's historic elite rise like a small city of the dead — one of the finest cemeteries in the Americas.

### Gotchas & Tips

- **The Currency Reality**: Cuba's monetary situation is genuinely complex. Bring more cash than you think you need — credit cards from US banks do not work, and ATM reliability is unpredictable. Euros or Canadian dollars are the most easily exchanged.
- **Internet Access**: WiFi in Cuba is limited to designated hotspot parks, sold via scratch cards. Embrace the disconnection — Havana rewards full attention.
- **Casa Particular vs Hotel**: Staying in a privately-run **casa particular** (B&B) puts you in direct contact with Havana's daily life and is usually both better and cheaper than the state-run hotels.

### Highlights

- **Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes**: One of Latin America's finest art museums, split between Cuban and international collections — the Cuban modern and contemporary works are particularly extraordinary.
- **El Floridita**: Hemingway's daiquiri bar has become more monument than bar, but the daiquiris are still genuinely good.
- **Fábrica de Arte Cubano**: Havana's creative centre — a former cooking oil factory converted into galleries, performance spaces, and bars. Open Thursday to Sunday, packed with Havana's young creative class.
- **Sunrise on the Malecón**: Before the city wakes, fishermen line the seawall and the light is completely unlike any other hour.

Havana does not try to be anything for you. It simply is what it is — and that turns out to be more than enough.
