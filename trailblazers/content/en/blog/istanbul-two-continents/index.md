![Istanbul, Turkey](cover.jpg)

The call to prayer from the Blue Mosque echoes across the Bosphorus and meets itself returning from the Asian shore. In Istanbul, this is not metaphor — the city genuinely straddles Europe and Asia, and the strait between them has been the axis around which empires turned for three millennia. Byzantine, Ottoman, and modern Turkish civilisation have each left their mark in stone, tile, and dome on the same hills, and the result is a city of staggering historical density that somehow also manages to be one of the most dynamic and contemporary cities in the world.

### Sultanahmet: The Historic Peninsula

The **Sultanahmet** district concentrates more history per square kilometre than almost anywhere on earth. The **Hagia Sophia** — originally a Byzantine cathedral, converted to a mosque in 1453, turned museum in the twentieth century, and mosque again in 2020 — is the pivot around which Istanbul's history revolves. Its central dome, floating on a ring of windows, creates an interior space that has awed visitors since AD 537. Directly opposite, the **Blue Mosque** (Sultanahmet Camii) dazzles with twenty thousand hand-painted İznik tiles in over fifty shades of blue. Between them, the **Hippodrome** still preserves the obelisks and column that once decorated the Byzantine chariot-racing track.

### The Bosphorus and the Grand Bazaar

The **Grand Bazaar** — with over 4,000 shops spread across 61 streets — is the world's oldest and largest covered market, and to enter it is to immediately understand why. The labyrinth of gold, leather, spices, ceramics, and carpets is simultaneously overwhelming and magnificent. Bargaining is expected, persistence required. For a contrast, the **Spice Bazaar** in Eminönü is smaller and more navigable, its stalls piled with saffron, dried apricots, Turkish delight, and the lokum that gives the whole market its sweet perfume. The **Bosphorus cruise** — bookable from Eminönü for a fraction of the tourist boat price — travels beneath three great bridges between two continents, past Ottoman waterfront palaces and fishing villages that seem unchanged for a century.

### Gotchas & Tips

- **Mosque Etiquette**: All mosques require shoes removed and shoulders and knees covered. Women need a headscarf. Most mosques provide these at the door, but bringing your own is preferable. Hagia Sophia's entry queues are severe — arrive before 8:30 AM or after 5 PM.
- **Neighbourhood Depth**: Sultanahmet is the historic core, but modern Istanbul's character lives in **Beyoğlu**, across the Golden Horn — the pedestrian İstiklal Avenue, the Galata Tower, and the galleries and restaurants of **Karaköy** are where Istanbul's contemporary creativity concentrates.
- **The Tea Question**: Turkish tea (çay) is served in small tulip-shaped glasses throughout the day. Accepting a glass when offered in a shop is not an obligation to buy — it is hospitality, and refusing is mildly impolite.

### Highlights

- **Topkapı Palace**: The administrative and ceremonial heart of the Ottoman Empire for four centuries — the treasury section alone, with its emerald-studded throne and jewelled daggers, takes two hours.
- **Chora Church (Kariye Camii)**: The finest Byzantine mosaics in existence — more intimate than Hagia Sophia and less visited.
- **Breakfast in Karaköy**: A full Turkish breakfast of cheeses, olives, honey, clotted cream, eggs, and simit is one of the world's great morning meals. The café culture of Karaköy does it best.
- **Sunset from Galata Tower**: The 360-degree view over both shores at golden hour is genuinely one of the world's great urban panoramas.

Istanbul is the city where East and West are not opposites but ingredients — mixed for three thousand years into something entirely its own.
